**Wei Zhongxian (魏忠贤)**  
- **Born:** 1559 (year 4 of the Jiajing Emperor’s reign) in Jiangsu province.  
- **Career:** He entered the imperial palace as a young eunuch and quickly rose through the ranks. By the early 1600s he had become the *chief eunuch* (大太监) of the court.  
- **Power & influence:** During the reign of the Tianqi Emperor (1620–1627) Wei exercised almost unchecked power over the imperial administration. He was the head of the infamous *Wei‑Yi‑Xian* faction—named after himself (Wei), the scholar‑official Wei Zhongxian’s colleague Yu Yiren (魏) and the imperial scholar Li Xian (廉)—which dominated policy, patronage, and the censorial system.  
- **Political machinations:** He orchestrated a series of purges against scholars, officials, and even members of the imperial clan who were perceived as threats. He was also notorious for monopolizing state revenues, running a vast private fortune, and employing bribery and intimidation to maintain his dominance.  
- **Downfall:** After the Tianqi Emperor’s death in 1627, the succeeding emperor (the Chongzhen Emperor) ordered a comprehensive purge of Wei’s network. Wei himself was imprisoned and executed in 1628. His tomb was later desecrated, and his name was erased from many official records, a classic example of *damnatio memoriae* in Chinese history.

**The Tianqi Emperor (Tianqi Huangdi)**  
- **Full name:** Zhu Jizhao (朱继淳).  
- **Reign:** 12 May 1620 – 11 February 1627 (age 17–24).  
- **Parents:** Son of the Wanli Emperor (Zhu Yong) and his primary consort, Consort Li.  
- **Rule:** The Tianqi reign is remembered mainly for the emperor’s inexperience and extreme reliance on his favorite eunuch, Wei Zhongxian. While the court was essentially controlled by Wei, the emperor himself rarely issued any orders, leading to a highly centralized eunuch bureaucracy.  
- **Policies:** Economic policy was largely neglected, leading to inflation, corruption, and worsening conditions for the peasantry. Militarily, the Ming forces were already in decline, and the emperor’s reign saw the loss of key frontier territories.  
- **Death:** He died of an illness at the age of 24. Historical records note that the emperor’s health was fragile and that his final days were spent largely under the supervision of Wei. His sudden death left a power vacuum that set the stage for the Chongzhen Emperor’s brief but decisive anti‑eunuch campaign.

**The Ming Dynasty (Ming Shu)**  
- **Founding:** Established by Zhu Yuanzhang (later Emperor Hongwu) in 1368 after the fall of the Yuan (Mongol) dynasty.  
- **Duration:** 1368 – 1644 (276 years).  
- **Structure:** The dynasty was a Confucian‑centered, agrarian empire that expanded its frontiers, strengthened its civil service system, and fostered a flourishing of arts and literature.  
- **Late‑dynastic decline:** From the 16th century onward, the Ming suffered from internal corruption, heavy taxation, and a breakdown of the agrarian economy. The powerful eunuch class, exemplified by Wei Zhongxian, exacerbated this decline by usurping state functions and creating factions that undermined official governance.  
- **Fall:** The Ming capitulated in 1644 when Li Zicheng’s peasant rebellion captured Beijing and the last Ming emperor, the Chongzhen Emperor, committed suicide. The Qing dynasty, ruled by the Manchus, then established its rule over China.
